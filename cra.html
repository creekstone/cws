What is CRA?

The Community Reinvestment Act (CRA) is a federal law enacted in 1977 to encourage depository institutions to help meet the credit needs of the communities in which they operate, including low- and moderate-income neighborhoods. The CRA requires federal banking regulators to assess how well each bank and thrift institution fulfills its obligations to these communities.

CRA examinations evaluate three main areas:

  • Lending Test: Assessment of a bank's record of helping to meet the credit needs of its assessment areas, particularly low- and moderate-income geographies and individuals
  • Investment Test: Evaluation of a bank's record of investing in community development projects and activities that benefit low- and moderate-income areas
  • Service Test: Review of a bank's record of providing retail banking services to low- and moderate-income individuals and communities

Why CRA Compliance Matters

CRA compliance is not just a regulatory requirement—it's an opportunity to demonstrate your institution's commitment to community development and responsible banking practices. Strong CRA performance can:

  • Enhance your institution's reputation in the community
  • Support business development and growth opportunities
  • Improve relationships with regulators and examiners
  • Create positive social impact in underserved communities
  • Provide competitive advantages in merger and acquisition activities
